# Less is more

- Remove repeated elements (e.g. repeated course name -> make it a subheading).
- Remove purely decorative lines, colours, backgrounds, animations, icons that compete for attention.
- No gradients anywhere (buttons, backgrounds, cards, icons, charts). Use flat solid fills.
- Avoid trend styles (glassmorphism, neumorphism) when they harm contrast or hierarchy.
- Text links: coloured and underlined by default. Cards, nav menus and tabs already look interactive, so they do not need link styling.
- Progressive disclosure: show what is needed now, reveal the rest on demand (expandable text, opt-in fields revealed by a checkbox). Use a descriptive link label to reveal.
- Minimal is not simple. Do not remove labels, selected states, contrast or key actions just to look clean. Unlabelled icon-only nav is a risk.
- Keep important content visible. If hiding is unavoidable, make it discoverable (e.g. show the edge of off-screen cards).
- Design the smallest screen first, then scale up. Do not fill large screens just because space exists.
- Reduce choice (Hick's Law) by: removing options, grouping/categorising, splitting into steps, recommending popular options. Keep autocomplete suggestions to about 10 or fewer.
